Project brief

From screen-time limits to practical alternatives.

Problem
Our team focused on the tension families can face around phone use: children may struggle to find something else to do, while parents may find it difficult to discuss boundaries calmly.
What we built
A chatbot with two roles: a playful “Quest Master” that proposes offline activities and a “Family Coach” that offers conversation scripts and family media-agreement ideas.
Tools
Poe, a custom system prompt, role-specific instructions, and conversational testing. The submitted lab did not identify the underlying model.
Result
In the documented child-mode simulation, the chatbot generated three offline quests and helped develop a game called “Jimmie Bimmie.” The test demonstrates a prototype interaction; it does not establish reduced screen time or improved family outcomes.
Interaction design

Two audiences. Different responses.

01 / CHILD MODE

Quest Master

Uses a playful tone and suggests offline activities based on the information the user supplies, such as available time and energy level.

Activity ideasCreative play
02 / PARENT MODE

Family Coach

Uses a calm tone and provides draft conversation scripts and media agreements for a parent to review and adapt.

Conversation supportHuman judgment
What worked in the lab

Fast conversational prototyping.

  • A custom system prompt allowed the team to build the prototype without application code.
  • Role-based instructions gave the assistant different tones and tasks for children and parents.
  • The team could test responses and revise the prompt within the same workflow.
Limitations and next steps

More testing before broader use.

  • Test longer conversations for context retention and ask users to confirm important details.
  • Recognize that suggestions depend on the family information supplied in the conversation.
  • Test the parent mode, age appropriateness, and responses to unexpected inputs.
  • Explore printable quest cards and a platform with explicit memory controls, with consent and privacy considered in the design.
Design process notes

  1. Empathize

    Considered the frustration children and parents may experience when screen-time boundaries become a source of conflict.

  2. Define

    Framed the need around engaging offline alternatives and supportive communication, rather than screen-time limits alone.

  3. Ideate

    Combined a child-facing activity guide with a parent-facing conversation coach in one two-mode assistant.

  4. Prototype

    Used a detailed Poe system prompt to define the personas, tone, and conditional instructions for each role.

  5. Test

    Simulated a child interaction and followed one suggested quest through to a collaboratively developed game.
